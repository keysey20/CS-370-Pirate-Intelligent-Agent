CS-370: Pirate Intelligent Agent

Project Overview

This project was completed as part of my CS-370 Current and Emerging Trends in Computer Science course at Southern New Hampshire University (SNHU). The objective was to develop an intelligent agent capable of navigating a maze and finding treasure using artificial intelligence and reinforcement learning.

The project introduced me to deep Q-learning, a technique that combines reinforcement learning with neural networks. Rather than following a predetermined route, the pirate agent learns through repeated experiences, evaluates different actions, and gradually improves its ability to reach the treasure.

Project Development: Provided Code and My Contributions

For this project, I was provided with starter code that established the maze environment and supporting functions. This included the TreasureMaze.py and GameExperience.py files, which handled the maze environment and stored experiences that could be used during training.

My primary responsibility was to complete the deep Q-learning training implementation within the Jupyter Notebook. I worked on the qtrain function, which trains the pirate agent through repeated episodes. The training process involved balancing exploration and exploitation, selecting actions, collecting rewards, updating the neural network, and evaluating the agent’s progress.

One of the challenges I encountered was understanding how different training parameters affected the agent’s performance. Working through the training process helped me develop a better understanding of how an intelligent system learns from its experiences instead of relying entirely on explicitly programmed instructions.

What Do Computer Scientists Do, and Why Does Their Work Matter?

Computer scientists study problems and develop computational solutions that can improve how people and organizations operate. Their work includes designing algorithms, developing software, analyzing data, improving cybersecurity, and creating intelligent systems.

I believe computer science is important because technology is now involved in almost every aspect of our daily lives, from healthcare and education to transportation, banking, and communication. Computer scientists help make these systems more efficient, secure, reliable, and accessible.

Throughout my studies, I have learned that computer science is not simply about writing code. It also involves understanding a problem, evaluating possible solutions, testing the results, and considering how technology will affect the people using it.

How Do I Approach a Problem as a Computer Scientist?

My approach begins with understanding the problem and identifying what the solution needs to accomplish. Before writing code, I try to break the problem into smaller parts and determine what tools, algorithms, or programming techniques would be most appropriate.

In the Pirate Intelligent Agent project, I had to understand how the maze environment worked, how the agent received rewards, and how the training process influenced its decisions. I then worked with the deep Q-learning implementation, tested its behavior, and observed how repeated training affected performance.

This project reinforced the importance of patience, troubleshooting, and continuous improvement. When something does not work as expected, I have learned to examine the code, review the results, and make adjustments rather than assume the first solution will always be correct.

My Ethical Responsibilities to End Users and Organizations

As a computer scientist, I have a responsibility to develop technology that is reliable, secure, fair, and respectful of the people who use it. This includes protecting personal information, identifying potential security risks, testing software thoroughly, and being transparent about what a system can and cannot do.

Artificial intelligence introduces additional ethical considerations because its decisions are influenced by training data, algorithms, and the conditions under which it operates. An AI system may produce incorrect or unexpected results, even when it appears to perform well during testing.

For organizations, I believe it is important to develop systems that support business objectives without compromising user privacy, security, or trust. Developers should also recognize potential biases, document limitations, and ensure appropriate human oversight when automated decisions could affect people’s lives.

The Pirate Intelligent Agent project helped me recognize that building an intelligent system requires more than achieving a successful result. It also requires understanding how that result was achieved and whether the system behaves responsibly.

Reflection

Completing this course has strengthened my understanding of artificial intelligence, neural networks, and reinforcement learning. Before working on this project, I understood AI primarily as technology capable of recognizing patterns and making predictions. Developing the pirate agent allowed me to see how an intelligent system can learn through interaction, feedback, and repeated training.

One of my biggest takeaways is that successful software development involves continuous learning. Even when a program runs successfully, there is always an opportunity to evaluate its performance, improve its efficiency, and consider alternative approaches.

As I continue developing my skills in computer science, I hope to apply what I have learned to real-world applications that solve meaningful problems and improve people’s experiences with technology.
